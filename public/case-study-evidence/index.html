What a BNB case study can and cannot establish

Review case-study reporting periods, total cash invested, operating costs and source documents.

Published by My BnB Accelerator, LLC · Company-owned resource
Home / What a BNB case study can and cannot establish

A single example is an example

A company case study can explain an acquisition approach or illustrate a transaction. It does not establish a typical result, the probability of a similar outcome or your future return. Property quality, purchase terms, operating choices and the period measured all influence what happened.

Start by recording what the example actually measures. A completed purchase, a furnished launch and a year of operations are distinct milestones. If the source does not identify the period or whether a result is projected, leave that field unresolved.

Reconcile the cash required

Request the components behind the total: down payment, closing costs, initial repairs, furnishing, service fees and reserves. Check whether any component was financed or omitted. A return based only on down payment can differ substantially from a return based on all cash committed.

Do not silently repair conflicting figures. Record both, identify the source and date, and ask the publisher which definition is intended. A consistent denominator is essential when comparing examples.

Trace the operating result

Look for the revenue period and a complete expense schedule. Ask whether management, cleaning, platform charges, utilities, insurance, property taxes, maintenance and debt payments are included. Identify amounts that are estimates rather than paid expenses.

Annualizing one strong month can hide seasonality. Compare a full operating cycle where available and account for owner stays, launch periods and temporary closures. Missing records limit the conclusion that can be drawn.

Keep the evidence boundaries visible

The company case-study library is the original source for its examples. This journal does not independently verify those results. Use the projection audit to request the records relevant to a particular purchase.
