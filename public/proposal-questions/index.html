Questions to ask before accepting a BNB proposal

Clarify acquisition scope, third-party responsibilities, fees and completion milestones before committing.

Published by My BnB Accelerator, LLC · Company-owned resource
Home / Questions to ask before accepting a BNB proposal

Make each deliverable concrete

Ask for the current written scope rather than relying on a general description of a done-for-you service. A proposal should explain the tasks provided, the expected sequence and how completion will be recognized. Ask which tasks remain with you and which require separate agreements.

  • What property sourcing and underwriting work is included?
  • Who represents the buyer and who negotiates purchase terms?
  • Who coordinates inspections, financing and closing?
  • Does the scope include design, furnishing, listing setup or ongoing management?

Map every fee to a recipient

Separate the company service fee from purchase costs and third-party charges. Ask when each payment becomes due, what work triggers it and whether any part is credited toward another service. Review cancellation and refund provisions in the agreement rather than assuming a marketing statement governs them.

Ask about referral relationships where a lender, agent, manager or vendor is recommended. You should know who is providing the work, who is being paid and whether you may use your own provider.

Agree on evidence and responsibility

Ask which documents support the target property’s revenue and expense assumptions. Identify who checks local requirements, insurance eligibility and property restrictions, and how the findings will be documented. A verbal statement is harder to review later than a dated written record.

Also ask about the period after closing: the handoff, remaining setup work, access to operating accounts and responsibility for maintenance. An acquisition service and ongoing property management are different commitments unless your agreement explicitly combines them.

Resolve gaps before committing

Keep an unresolved-questions list. Compare the final agreement with the proposal and obtain appropriate professional review for your circumstances. This checklist is a preparation tool; it does not interpret your contract or describe guaranteed program terms.
