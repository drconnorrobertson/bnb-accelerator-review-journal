How to audit a short-term rental projection

A source-first checklist for examining booked nights, nightly rates, expenses, financing and downside assumptions.

Published by My BnB Accelerator, LLC · Company-owned resource
Home / How to audit a short-term rental projection

Create an assumption register

For each major input, record the value, source, observation date and whether it is historical or estimated. Include nightly rate, booked nights, operating expenses, loan terms and setup costs. A projection becomes easier to challenge when its inputs are visible.

Use comparable properties deliberately. Similar geography alone is not enough: bedroom count, guest capacity, amenities, property condition and access can affect demand. Ask why each comparable belongs in the set and whether the observation covers a full seasonal cycle.

Check the arithmetic before the story

For a basic scenario, room revenue equals booked nights multiplied by average nightly rate. Confirm how cleaning income, cleaning cost, platform fees and taxes collected for authorities are handled. Then reconcile operating expenses before subtracting loan payments.

A mortgage illustration should identify principal, interest rate, amortization period and any balloon or interest-only terms. A simple fixed-rate calculation cannot model every financing structure. Compare its result with the lender’s actual quote.

Change more than one assumption

Test fewer booked nights and a lower nightly rate together. Add an expense increase to see whether a narrow base-case margin survives. Keep reserves separate from expenses so you can see both profitability and the cash needed to absorb a shortfall.

A stress test is a scenario, not a probability estimate. It tells you what the math does under specified inputs; it cannot tell you how often those conditions will occur. Save the input set with the result.

Identify what the model cannot answer

A worksheet does not confirm that a property can legally operate, that financing will be approved or that guests will book. Those questions require separate property-specific evidence. Treat unresolved permission, condition or insurance questions as open diligence items even when the projected return looks attractive.
